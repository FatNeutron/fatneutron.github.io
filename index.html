research

Most seismology uses the signals from earthquakes. I use the background noise that usually gets thrown away: the weak, constant vibration from ocean waves, wind and traffic that every seismometer records.

If you cross-correlate the noise recorded at two stations over many days, it turns into a wave travelling from one station to the other, as if one of them were the source. The line at the top of this page shows this. One day of noise looks random, and after a year of stacking the wave is clear.

I use these waves to measure dv/v, the small changes in seismic velocity in the ground between two stations. These changes follow things like groundwater, temperature and stress, and velocity drops have been seen days before volcanic eruptions.

For seismologists: ambient noise cross-correlation and dv/v monitoring from coda waves, in Python with ObsPy. My thesis reproduces the dv/v results of Author et al. (20XX).
